The weight question

There's a moment when you first pick up a black granite Urli that surprises almost everyone. You expect heaviness — it's stone, after all — but the specific quality of the weight is not what you anticipated. It doesn't feel like a burden. It feels like resolution.

This is the central fact about granite as a domestic material: its weight is not punitive. It communicates permanence without demanding you acknowledge it. The object sits, and in sitting, gives the room a quiet authority that lighter objects simply cannot provide.

"Weight in an object is like silence in conversation — it gives the other elements room to breathe."

Where it comes from

Our black granite comes from a single quarry outside Jalore, in southern Rajasthan. The seam we use was identified by our sourcing partner seven years ago — it's finer-grained than most commercial black granite, which means it takes a polish without losing its matte warmth.

What looks uniform to the eye is actually a dense interlocking of quartz, feldspar, and biotite mica — it's the mica that gives black granite its occasional glint, that moment when a surface that seemed uniformly dark suddenly catches light and reveals itself as a constellation of tiny mirrors.

The quarry in Jalore, southern Rajasthan — early morning, before the cutting begins.

How it's made

The Urli shape is first roughed out on a diamond-tipped lathe — a process that takes about forty minutes for a large bowl. What emerges looks like the right shape but the wrong material: pale where the stone has been freshly cut, dusty with grinding residue.

The hand-work begins after. A team of three chisels, then sands, then polishes — each stage with progressively finer abrasive — until the interior reaches a finish smooth enough to hold water without weeping through the grain. The exterior is left with a slightly rougher texture: intentional, honest, a reminder that this was once part of a mountain.

Living with it

People use the Urli in ways we didn't anticipate. Some fill it with water and float seasonal flowers. Some keep it dry, using it as a centrepiece that holds nothing — and find that it works precisely because of that emptiness. Some use it outdoors, which it handles without complaint.

The object asks very little of you in return for what it gives. Wipe it occasionally. Oil it once a year if you want to maintain the sheen. Otherwise: let it sit, and let it work.

Our story

Made with intention.

We are a small studio in Mumbai that believes objects should earn their place in a room. Everything we carry is chosen for how it sits, not just how it looks.

What we believe

"We don't curate collections. We curate the materials that make a house remember it is a home."

Elements of Decor · Est. 2022 · Mumbai

The rules of the room.

01

Objects should earn their place

Nothing we carry exists simply to fill a shelf. Each piece is chosen for what it does to a room — the weight it adds, the silence it creates, the question it asks.

02

Material is the message

Stone, metal, wood, ceramic — these are not finishes. They are the substance of the thing. We care where the stone was quarried, how the wood was dried, who polished the metal.

03

Handmade means imperfect means honest

Variation in a hand-thrown pot is not a defect. It is the record of the maker's hand. We celebrate that trace rather than demanding uniformity that only machines can deliver.

04

Less, but considered

We carry fewer things than most home stores. Deliberately. An object that has been chosen carefully is worth twenty that were chosen hastily.

05

The corner is not dead space

Across Indian traditions, corners and thresholds have always been the first places to receive a sacred or beautiful object. We design our curation around this idea.

06

A home is not a show home

We don't sell aspirational interiors. We sell individual objects that work in real rooms, with real light, lived in by real people who don't style for photographs.

How we work.

01

We source at the material

Every category starts with the raw material — stone, metal, clay, wood. We visit quarries, kilns, and workshops before we visit showrooms. The origin shapes every decision after.

02

We commission, not purchase

Most of what we carry is made specifically for us — to our dimensions, our finish specifications, our tolerances. We work with craftspeople over seasons, not orders.

03

We test in a real room

Every new object lives in our studio for at least one month before it goes live. We watch how it holds light across the day, how it changes with the season, what it asks for next to it.

Our studio in Bandra Kurla Complex is open by appointment. We don't have a showroom in the traditional sense — it's a working space where you can see objects in situ, ask questions, and handle what we carry before buying.
